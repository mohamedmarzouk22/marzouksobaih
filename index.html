About

I am a computational chemist working on the physics side of drug discovery: where a ligand binds, how tightly, and why. My toolbox is all-atom molecular dynamics, enhanced sampling and free-energy calculations, applied to protein–ligand, protein–DNA, protein–protein and receptor–ligand systems.

I trained as a biophysicist at Ain Shams University (B.Sc. 2009, ranked first in class), then moved to Japan in 2019 on an Egypt–Japan Education Partnership scholarship for a Ph.D. at Tokyo Institute of Technology. Since then I have worked at QST, led an independent JSPS KAKENHI project at WPI-PRIMe (The University of Osaka), and, since July 2026, applied the same methods in industry R&D at TechnoPro R&D.

What drives the work is simple: simulations are only useful if they change a decision. I build pipelines that are validated against known answers, reproducible on a cluster, and clear enough that a chemist can act on the result.
